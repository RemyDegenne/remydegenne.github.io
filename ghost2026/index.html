Fermat and Navier-Stokes

September 4 2026: Anthropic announces a full formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem

Known maths. 13M lines of code. $300k?

September 8 2026: OpenAI announces a solution to the Navier-Stokes problem, with a Lean proof.

New solution to a millennium problem. 400k lines of code. $10M+?

What does the Lean proof of Navier-Stokes bring?

  • Nobody will read the 400k lines

  • Many people compiled it: Lean accepts the proof

  • Experts read the theorem and important definitions: it proves the right thing

Little more than 1 bit of information: the proof is correct.

All OpenAI needs: their AI can solve Navier-Stokes!

Not what we want: the pdf is slop, and the code is not better.

Lean as a filter for AI output

AI generates many proof candidates, very fast. Some are wrong. Verification is the bottleneck.

An AI review will prune some wrong candidates, but not all.

A Lean proof will (if it proves the right thing).

It costs millions!

To solve a Clay Millennium problem, yes.

To answer a question about a lemma from day-to-day research and give a Lean-backed answer, no.

  • Last week, Claude Opus 5.5 solved in 40 minutes a problem I was stuck on for 2 years

  • I formalize my preprints as I write them

Getting started

You need:

  1. A Lean project that imports Mathlib, optionally an AI subscription

  2. Mathematical knowledge, and a question or a written proof

  3. Some amount of Lean knowledge

Your experience will depend a lot on those three factors.

Outputs

You will get some of the following:

  1. Feedback on your proof: "I can't formalize this, because it's false". "I proved lemma X in a simpler way".

  2. A full proof of a theorem, perhaps the one you wanted.

  3. Proofs of many things that were missing from the libraries.

  4. Insight into your proof and the literature.

Why formalize mathematics?

  • verification: check correctness, trust

  • understanding: search the library, explore the proof

  • creation: remove a hypothesis and see what breaks, find generalizations, use automation

  • collaboration: leverage the trust to work together

Wrong definitions

def IsSubGaussianProcess (μ : Measure Ω) (X : E → Ω → ℝ) (σ : ℝ) : Prop := ∀ s t : E, ∀ l : ℝ, ∫ ω, exp (l * (X s ω - X t ω)) ∂μ ≤ exp (l^2 * σ^2 * (dist s t)^2 / 2)

What if \exp(l (X_s - X_t)) is not integrable?

The integral is 0 by definition. Not what we want.

Lean checks proofs, it does not read your mind.

Quality issues

  • restricted definition (perhaps the general one requires too much background)

  • too many hypotheses (need to be checked at every use)

  • slow, inefficient code

  • unmaintainable, brittle code

  • lack of understanding

  • lack of attribution
